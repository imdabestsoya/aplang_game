### P1 — Trace the poppet

At card `needle-04`:

- **First event:** Mary makes the poppet in court
- **Second event:** The poppet reaches Elizabeth
- **Third event:** The object is interpreted as evidence against Elizabeth
- **What does possession establish?:** Possession alone does not establish intent

Order the making, gift and later use of the object as evidence. Mary’s needle account is testimony, not proof of Elizabeth’s intent. The exercise does not require an exact needle-placement time.

### P2 — Examine the alleged motive

At card `court-02`:

- **Property-interest note:** Observation
- **Giles’s claim:** Allegation
- **Court claim:** Inference
- **What follows from a possible motive?:** A possible motive merits scrutiny but is not proved by allegation alone

The constructed transfer is an observation within an invented artifact; Giles offers an allegation; the court draws an inference. Motive invites scrutiny, not a verdict.

### P3 — Test the court’s rule

At card `court-04`:

- **How does the court treat an accusation?:** An accusation establishes suspicion
- **How does the court treat defense?:** Defending the accused creates more suspicion
- **What is the logical result?:** Counterargument becomes confirmation; meaningful disproof is impossible

A rule that treats both accusation and defense as guilt allows no disproof. Identifying that contradiction challenges the rule, not just one verdict.
